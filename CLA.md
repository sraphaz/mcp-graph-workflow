## 9. How to Sign

**Option A — Automated (recommended for most contributors).**
The Project uses the `contributor-assistant` (cla-assistant.io) GitHub
Action. The first time You open a pull request, the bot will comment with a
link asking You to sign the CLA. Follow the link, authenticate with GitHub,
and click **"I agree"**. Your signature is recorded alongside Your GitHub
username.

**Option B — Manual.**
Email a signed copy of this Agreement to the Project Owner via the contact
channel listed in [`COMMERCIAL.md`](COMMERCIAL.md), with the subject line
`CLA signature — <your GitHub username>`. Include Your full legal name, a
stable email address, and (optionally) Your employer if covered by Section
4.3.

**Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO).**
Independent of the CLA, every commit You submit to the Project must include
a `Signed-off-by:` trailer (`git commit --signoff` or `-s`). This attests
that You meet the terms of the
[Developer Certificate of Origin v1.1](https://developercertificate.org/) —
essentially, that You have the right to submit the Contribution under the
Project's open-source license. The DCO is enforced by the commit-msg hook
and by CI.

**The DCO is not a substitute for the CLA.** The DCO attests to the origin
of the Contribution; the CLA additionally grants the Project Owner the
sublicensing rights needed to sustain the commercial tier. Contributions
received with a DCO sign-off but no CLA signature are accepted only under
AGPL-3.0-or-later and **are not relicensed into the commercial tier**. If
You prefer this arrangement — contribute under AGPL only, without enabling
the commercial sublicensing — simply do not sign the CLA and explicitly
decline when the bot asks. The Project Owner may still merge Your
Contribution at their discretion.
